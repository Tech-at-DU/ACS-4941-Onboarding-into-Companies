## Why you should know this

An informational interview is a short conversation with someone working in a role or company you're interested in. It's the bridge between outreach and a referral. Behavioral interviews ("Tell me about a time when…") are part of nearly every interview loop, and they're won by preparation: a bank of real stories you can adapt to any question.

## Learning Objectives

1. Run an informational interview that builds a relationship and leads naturally toward a referral
1. Structure behavioral answers using STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
1. Build a story bank of 8 or more stories that cover common behavioral themes
1. Give and receive structured feedback as candidate, interviewer, and observer

## Standup (10 min)

What I did · What I'll do · Where I'm stuck. Outreach numbers: messages sent, replies so far. Celebrate any replies.

## Overview: Informational Interviews (15 min)

**Purpose:** learn about the role, team, and company — and build a relationship. Not to ask for a job directly.

**Structure for a 20-minute call:**

1. Thank them and confirm the time (1 min)
1. Your 30-second intro (1 min)
1. Your questions (15 min) — see the [question ideas](../Assignments/Networking_Outreach.md#informational-interview-question-ideas)
1. Close (3 min): "Is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to?" and, if it went well, "I'm planning to apply to [role] — would you be open to me following up about it?"

**After:** thank-you note within 24 hours, mentioning something specific they said. Log it in your tracker.

**Good question to always ask now:** "How does your team use AI tools day to day?" — you'll learn what employers actually expect, and it gives you material for your own interviews.

## Activity: Informational Interview Role-Play (30 min)

Three rounds of 10 minutes. The instructor plays an engineer at a company each student has chosen, with a different personality each round (friendly and chatty, busy and brief, quiet and reserved).

- **Candidate:** runs the call, including the close.
- **Observer:** notes: Was the intro under 30 seconds? Were the questions specific? Did they ask for further introductions?
- 2 minutes of feedback after each round.

## Overview: STAR & Story Banks (20 min)

Walk through the [Behavioral Interview Guide](../Resources/Behavioral_Interview_Guide.md):

- **STAR:** Situation (brief) → Task (your responsibility) → Action (what *you* did — most of your answer) → Result (with a number and a lesson).
- **Story bank:** 8–10 stories that each cover several questions. Prepare stories, not scripts.
- **Themes to cover:** technical challenge, failure, conflict, learning fast, initiative, feedback, deadlines, helping others, and **AI use — including a time it went wrong**.
- **Common mistakes:** "we" instead of "I," too much setup, no result.

Model one weak and one strong answer to "Tell me about a time you failed."

## Activity: Build Your Story Bank (35 min)

1. List 8–10 stories in the [story bank table](../Resources/Behavioral_Interview_Guide.md#your-story-bank) (10 min). Pull from projects, past jobs (tech or not), school, and volunteering.
1. Check the theme coverage list. Fill gaps (5 min).
1. Write out 2 stories in full STAR form (20 min).

**AI in practice:** You can paste your rough notes for one story into an AI tool and ask it to arrange them in STAR order and point out what's missing (like a measurable result). Then rewrite it in your own words — and check it didn't add anything that didn't happen. Interviewers ask follow-ups, and invented details fall apart.

## First Mock Triad: Behavioral (30 min)

With three students, the whole class forms one triad. Three rounds of 10 minutes, rotating roles (see [Mock Interview Loop](../Assignments/Mock_Interview_Loop.md)):

- **Interviewer:** asks "Tell me about yourself" plus one behavioral question from the [question bank](../Resources/Behavioral_Interview_Guide.md#question-bank), with one follow-up.
- **Candidate:** answers using STAR.
- **Observer:** scores Opener, STAR structure, and Communication on the rubric and keeps time.

Record your answers on your phone (with permission) for self-review later.

## Challenge 3 Assigned: Take-Home Without AI (10 min)
