# Behavioral Interview Guide

Behavioral questions ask about how you've handled real situations: "Tell me about a time when…". Interviewers use them to predict how you'll act on the job. For junior candidates they often decide the outcome as much as the technical round does.

---

## The STAR method

STAR stands for **Situation, Task, Action, Result**. It's a simple structure that keeps your answers focused.

| Part | What to say | Share of your answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Situation** | The context, in one or two sentences. Where, when, who. | ~15% |
| **Task** | What *you* were responsible for, or the problem to solve. | ~10% |
| **Action** | What *you* did, step by step. Say "I," not "we." This is the most important part. | ~60% |
| **Result** | What happened, with a number if possible, plus what you learned. | ~15% |

Aim for about **2 minutes** per answer. Shorter for AI screens and recorded interviews (60–90 seconds).

### Example

> **Q: Tell me about a time you had to learn something quickly.**
>
> **S:** In my final term, my team was building a group project app and our backend developer left the program two weeks before our demo.
> **T:** I'd only done frontend work, but I volunteered to take over the API.
> **A:** I spent the first day reading his code and writing down every endpoint and what it did. I used an AI assistant to explain the parts of the Express middleware I didn't understand, then verified by writing small tests for each route. I found two endpoints that didn't validate input and fixed them. I set up daily 15-minute check-ins with the team so frontend work wasn't blocked on me.
> **R:** We shipped on time, the demo ran without errors, and I added backend work to my resume. I also learned that writing tests is the fastest way for me to understand unfamiliar code.

Aim to cover all of these themes across your stories:

- [ ] A technical challenge you solved
- [ ] A time you failed or made a mistake
- [ ] A conflict or disagreement with a teammate
- [ ] Learning something new quickly
- [ ] Taking initiative or leading without a title
- [ ] Getting or acting on difficult feedback
- [ ] Working under a deadline or with ambiguity
- [ ] Helping someone else succeed
- [ ] A time you used AI tools — including when it went wrong
- [ ] Why this field / why you're changing careers (if relevant)

## Question bank

### Openers

- Tell me about yourself. *(Practice a 60–90 second version: present, past, future.)*
- Why are you interested in this role / this company?
- Walk me through your resume.
- What's a project you're proud of? *(Use your proof-of-work project.)*

### Teamwork & conflict

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate. How did you handle it?
- Describe a time you worked with someone who was difficult to work with.
- Tell me about a time you had to give someone critical feedback.

### Growth & failure

- Tell me about a time you failed.
- Describe a piece of feedback that changed how you work.
- What's something you're working on improving right now?

### Ownership & ambiguity

- Tell me about a time you took on something outside your job description.
- Describe a time you had to make a decision without all the information.
- Tell me about a time you missed a deadline or almost did.

### Remote & asynchronous work

- How do you stay productive and visible when working remotely?
- Tell me about a time a misunderstanding happened over chat or email. How did you fix it?
- How do you decide whether to ask a question or keep trying on your own?

### AI and how you work

These are now common at almost every company. Have honest, specific answers ready.

- How do you use AI tools in your development work?
- Tell me about a time an AI tool gave you a wrong answer. How did you catch it?
- How do you make sure you understand code an AI wrote for you?
- When do you choose *not* to use AI?

**Strong answers** are specific and show judgment: which tool, what for, how you verified, and where you draw the line. **Weak answers** are either "I don't use AI" (often reads as out of date) or "I use it for everything" (reads as unable to work without it).

### Closing

- Do you have any questions for us? *(Always have three ready. Good examples below.)*

---

## Questions to ask your interviewer

- What does a successful first 90 days look like in this role?
- How does the team review code, and how are new engineers onboarded?
- What's the team's approach to AI tools — which ones do you use and how?
- What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?
- What do you enjoy most about working here?
- What are the next steps in the process, and when should I expect to hear back?

---

## Common mistakes

- Saying "we" for the whole answer so the interviewer can't tell what you did
- Spending most of the time on the situation and rushing the action
- No result, or a result with no number or lesson
- Badmouthing a former teammate, employer, or instructor
- Memorizing answers word-for-word so they sound robotic
- Making up a story — follow-up questions expose it quickly
