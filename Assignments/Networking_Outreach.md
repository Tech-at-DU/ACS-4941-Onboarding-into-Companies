# Networking, Outreach & Informational Interviews

**Weight:** 10% · **Ongoing** · **Final log due:** Session 11 (Mon 11/30)

## Why

Most jobs, especially entry-level ones in a tight market, are filled through people: referrals, introductions, and relationships. A referral can make you many times more likely to get an interview than a cold application. This assignment builds the habit of reaching out every week.

## Requirements

### If you're job seeking

By Session 11, log in your tracker:

- [ ] At least **40 outreach messages** (about 10 per week from Week 2 on)
- [ ] At least **3 informational interviews** (15–30 minute conversations with people working in roles or companies you're interested in)
- [ ] At least **2 referral requests** to people you've built some connection with
- [ ] A thank-you note sent within 24 hours after every conversation
- [ ] Follow-ups on unanswered messages after about one week

### If you're already employed

- [ ] At least **3 informational interviews** with engineers at other companies or in other teams
- [ ] At least **15 outreach messages** to grow your professional network
- [ ] A short reflection on what you learned about other teams' practices

## Who to reach out to (warmest first)

"Warm" outreach means you share some connection with the person. Warm messages get far more replies than cold ones.

1. ACS alumni and instructors' contacts
2. People you've met at events, meetups, or hackathons
3. Second-degree LinkedIn connections (friends of friends) — ask for an introduction
4. People in communities you belong to (Discord, open source projects, bootcamps, clubs)
5. Engineers at your target companies who've written posts or given talks you can mention
6. Cold contacts (lowest reply rate — still worth some messages)

## Outreach templates

Use these as starting points. **Always personalize the bolded parts.** A message that could be sent to anyone gets ignored.

### Alumni / shared background

> Hi **Sam** — I'm finishing the Applied Computer Science program at **[school]** and saw you went through it too. I'm looking at **backend roles at fintech companies** and noticed you're at **Plaid**. Would you be open to a 15-minute chat about how you got started there? Happy to work around your schedule.

### Mentioning their work

> Hi **Priya** — I read your post on **how your team migrated to TypeScript** and the part about **typing API responses** helped me on my own project. I'm a new developer looking at **full-stack roles**. Could I ask you a couple of questions about your team's work sometime? 15 minutes would be great.

### Asking for a referral (after you've talked)

> Hi **Sam** — thanks again for the chat last week. I applied to the **Junior Backend Engineer** role (link) and think it's a strong fit because **I built a payments dashboard with Postgres and Stripe's API**. Would you feel comfortable referring me? I've attached my resume and can send a short blurb to make it easy.

### Follow-up

> Hi **Priya** — just bumping this in case it got buried. Totally understand if you're busy!

## Using AI for outreach

- **Good:** asking an AI to help you find common ground with someone based on their public profile, or to shorten a draft you wrote.
- **Bad:** sending AI-written messages unedited. They sound generic and people can tell.
- **Rule:** you write the final message. Keep it under 100 words.

## Informational interview question ideas

- How did you get into this role?
- What does a typical week look like for you?
- What do you look for in junior engineers on your team?
- How does your team use AI tools day to day?
- What do you wish you'd known in your first year?
- Is there anyone else you'd recommend I talk to?
