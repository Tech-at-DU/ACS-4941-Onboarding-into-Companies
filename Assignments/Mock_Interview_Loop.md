# Behavioral & Full Mock Interview Loop

**Weight:** 15% · **Peer mock triads:** Weeks 3–6 · **Graded full loop:** Session 12 (Wed 12/2)

## Why

Interviewing is a performance skill. The only way to get good is reps with feedback. This assignment combines weekly peer practice with one full, graded mock loop that simulates a real final round.

## Part 1: Peer mock triads (Weeks 3–6)

With three students, the whole class forms one triad. In each round, everyone rotates through three roles:

| Role | Job |
| --- | --- |
| **Candidate** | Answers questions as in a real interview |
| **Interviewer** | Asks questions from the [Behavioral Interview Guide](../Resources/Behavioral_Interview_Guide.md) or [Problem Bank](../Resources/Problem_Bank.md), plus follow-ups |
| **Observer** | Scores the candidate on the rubric below and keeps time |

Rotate so everyone plays every role. Record the candidate's answers (with permission) so they can review themselves later. Playing the interviewer teaches you what interviewers actually notice.

**Before each graded loop, self-review one recording:** watch it, score yourself on the rubric, and note two things to fix.

## Part 2: Full mock loop (Session 12)

Each student completes a 45-minute loop with the instructor (and an alum or guest interviewer when available):

| Segment | Time | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opener | 5 min | "Tell me about yourself" + "Why this role?" |
| Project deep-dive | 10 min | Walk through your proof-of-work project; expect "why did you…?" questions |
| Technical | 15 min | One problem from the Problem Bank — algorithm, debugging, or code review (chosen by the instructor) |
| Behavioral | 10 min | Two or three behavioral questions, including one about how you use AI |
| Your questions | 5 min | You ask the interviewer questions |

Bring a target job description. The instructor will tailor the loop to it.

## Rubric (used for triads and the graded loop)

Score 1–4 on each row.

| Criteria | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Opener** | Rambling or unprepared | Clear but long or generic | Concise and tailored to the role | Concise, tailored, and memorable |
| **Project deep-dive** | Can't explain decisions | Explains what, not why | Explains decisions and tradeoffs | Explains decisions, tradeoffs, and what they'd change; handles curveballs |
| **Technical** | See the [challenge rubric](Technical_Challenges.md#rubric) | | | |
| **STAR structure** | No structure | Partial (missing action or result) | Clear STAR, mostly "I" | Clear STAR, specific actions, measurable result, lesson learned |
| **AI question** | Vague or evasive | Generic answer | Specific tools, uses, and verification | Specific, shows judgment about when *not* to use AI |
| **Communication** | Hard to follow | Understandable | Clear and confident | Clear, confident, and engaging; adapts to the interviewer |
| **Questions for interviewer** | None | Generic | Thoughtful | Thoughtful and specific to the company |

## Grading

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Participated in all peer mock rounds in all three roles | 20 |
| Self-review of one recording submitted before Session 12 | 10 |
| Full mock loop: average rubric score of 2.5 or higher | 60 |
| Written reflection after the loop: top 3 strengths and top 3 things to work on | 10 |
