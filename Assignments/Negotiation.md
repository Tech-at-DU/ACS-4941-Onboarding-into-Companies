# Offer Evaluation & Negotiation

**Weight:** 5% · **Due:** Session 13 (Mon 12/7, in class)

## Why

A few minutes of negotiation can raise your first salary — and every raise after it builds on that number. Evaluating an offer well also helps you avoid accepting a job that looks good on paper but isn't (unstable startup, bad contract terms, no visa support). Study the [Salary Negotiation Guide](../Resources/Salary_Negotiation_Guide.md) first.

## Part 1: Research (bring to Session 13)

For one real job posting you're interested in, write down:

- [ ] Your walk-away, target, and anchor salary numbers, with at least two sources for each
- [ ] Three questions you'd ask about the full package (equity, benefits, RTO / return-to-office policy, etc.)
- [ ] If relevant to you: your work authorization questions for the employer

## Part 2: Live negotiation role-play (in class)

The instructor plays a recruiter making you an offer. You'll get one of these scenarios (assigned at random):

1. **Standard offer** — salary below your target, everything else fine
2. **Startup offer** — lower salary, stock options, short deadline
3. **Contract-to-hire** — hourly 1099 contract with vague conversion terms
4. **Fixed salary** — the recruiter says base salary can't change

Then practice with a classmate playing the recruiter, using an AI tool to generate a new scenario for each other.

## Part 3: Verbal assessment

Answer questions from Section 7 of the [Salary Negotiation Guide](../Resources/Salary_Negotiation_Guide.md#7-verbal-assessment-questions). Answer at least 70% correctly to pass.

## Grading

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Research complete with sourced numbers | 25 |
| Role-play: thanked, asked for time, made a reasoned ask, didn't accept on the spot | 40 |
| Verbal assessment: 70% or more correct | 35 |
