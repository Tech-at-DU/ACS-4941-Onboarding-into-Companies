## Why you should know this

The first round of many interview processes is now a screen run by AI: a voice on a phone call, a chat window, or a recorded video interview scored partly by software. It's strange the first time, and the first time shouldn't be a real one. Today you'll practice with free AI tools, with classmates observing and giving human feedback alongside the AI's.

## Learning Objectives

1. Describe the formats of first-round screens: recruiter calls, AI phone and chat screens, and recorded (async) video interviews
1. Give concise, structured answers suited to AI-scored and recorded formats
1. Handle salary and logistics questions confidently in a first screen
1. Compare AI-generated interview feedback with human feedback and judge which to trust
1. Reflect on the strengths and limits of AI-run hiring

## Overview: First-Round Screens (15 min)

Read together: [AI in the Job Search, Section 4](../Resources/AI_In_The_Job_Search.md#4-ai-run-interviews-ai-screens).

- **What a first screen checks:** Are you a real, qualified candidate? Can you communicate clearly? Are salary, location, work authorization, and timing compatible?
- **Formats:**
  - **Recruiter call** — a human, 15–30 minutes, mostly about fit and logistics
  - **AI phone screen** — an AI voice asks similar questions, sometimes basic technical ones
  - **AI chat screen** — the same by text
  - **Async video interview** — you record answers to preset questions, often with 30 seconds to prepare, 1–2 minutes to answer, and limited retakes
- **How to do well:** 60–90 second answers, lead with the point, one example, steady pace, notes for facts but no script, relevant language from the job posting.
- **Your rights:** some places (Illinois, New York City) require employers to tell you when AI is used in hiring decisions. It's fine to ask a recruiter, "Is this screen reviewed by a person?"
- **Salary question:** have a researched range ready (preview of [Session 13](Lesson13.md)).

## Setup: Tools, Prompts & Privacy (10 min)

Everyone opens a free AI voice tool (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini app voice mode). Alternatives: Google Interview Warmup, or text mode plus reading answers aloud.

- **Privacy check:** review data settings; turn off training on your conversations if you prefer. Don't share real personal identifiers during practice.
- Copy the screen prompt from the [AI Screen Practice assignment](../Assignments/AI_Screen_Practice.md#part-1-run-three-ai-screens-two-in-class-one-on-your-own) and fill in a real target role and company type.

**Instructor note:** test one voice tool on the classroom Wi-Fi before class. If voice mode isn't available on someone's device, they can use text mode on a laptop and speak their answers aloud to the observers.

## AI Screen Lab, Round 1: Live AI Phone Screens (45 min)

One student at a time (about 15 minutes each), on speaker so everyone can hear:

1. **Candidate** runs the 10-minute AI phone screen.
1. **Observers** score the candidate on the [observer rubric](../Assignments/AI_Screen_Practice.md#observer-rubric) while listening.
1. When the AI gives its score, observers share their scores. Where do the AI and the humans agree? Disagree?

## AI Screen Lab, Round 2: Recorded Video Screens (30 min)

1. Everyone records at the same time (in separate spaces if possible) for 15 minutes: generate 3 questions with the AI, then record each answer with 30 seconds to prepare, 90 seconds to answer, no retakes.
1. Watch one clip from each student together (15 minutes). What did you notice watching yourself that you didn't notice while answering? Eye contact, filler words, pace, length?

## Debrief: AI Feedback vs. Human Feedback (10 min)

- What did the AI notice that humans didn't, and vice versa?
- What would you trust AI feedback for (length, structure, filler words)? What wouldn't you trust it for (whether you came across as genuine, whether your story was compelling)?
- How did it *feel* to be interviewed by an AI? How should you prepare differently for a real one?
