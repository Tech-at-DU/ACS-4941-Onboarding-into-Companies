# AI Screen Practice

**Weight:** 5% · **In class:** Session 6 (Wed 11/4) · **Due:** Session 7 (Mon 11/9)

## Why

Many companies now use AI to run first-round interviews: an AI voice on a phone call, an AI chat, or a recorded video interview reviewed by software. It feels strange the first time. Practicing it a few times removes most of the weirdness and helps you learn to give concise, structured answers. Read [AI in the Job Search, Section 4](../Resources/AI_In_The_Job_Search.md#4-ai-run-interviews-ai-screens) first.

## Free tools you can use

You don't need to pay for anything. Free tiers change, so check what's available before class:

- **AI voice modes** in free mobile apps: ChatGPT voice mode, Claude voice mode, Google Gemini Live
- **Google Interview Warmup** — a free Google practice tool that asks interview questions and transcribes your answers
- **Your laptop camera and any recording app** — for practicing recorded (async) video interviews

**Privacy:** don't share personal identifiers (Social Security number, address, date of birth) with these tools. Use a made-up phone number or "I'd share that with the recruiter directly" if asked. Check your app's data settings and turn off training on your conversations if you prefer.

## Part 1: Run three AI screens (two in class, one on your own)

Use this prompt in an AI voice mode. Replace the bracketed parts.

```text
You are an AI recruiter conducting a 10-minute first-round phone screen for
a [Junior Full-Stack Developer] role at [a 200-person healthcare software
company]. Ask me one question at a time and wait for my full answer. Ask
about: my background, why I'm interested in this role, a project I'm proud
of, one simple technical question about [JavaScript], my salary expectations,
and my availability. Don't give feedback until the end. At the end, score me
1-5 on clarity, relevance, and conciseness, and tell me the two things I
should improve most.
```

Run three screens:

1. **Phone screen** (in class) — voice only, the prompt above
2. **Recorded video screen** (in class) — use the prompt below in text mode to get 3 questions, then record yourself answering each on camera with 30 seconds to prepare and 90 seconds to answer, no retakes

   ```text
   Give me 3 questions a company might ask in a recorded video interview for a
   [junior developer] role. Show them one at a time when I say "next."
   ```

3. **A screen for a role outside your comfort zone** (on your own, before Session 7) — try a QA (quality assurance) / SDET (software development engineer in test), solutions engineer, or FDE (forward-deployed engineer) role

In class, classmates observe and score each screen using the rubric below, alongside the AI's own score.

## Part 2: Reflection (due Session 7)

Write a short reflection (half a page) in your repo or tracker:

1. Paste the AI's feedback from one screen.
2. Compare it with your classmates' feedback. Where did they agree? Where did the AI miss something a human noticed (or the other way around)?
3. What two changes will you make to your answers?
4. Re-run one screen after making those changes. Did your score improve?
5. How did it feel talking to an AI interviewer, and what will you do differently in a real one?

## Observer rubric

| Criteria | 1 (needs work) | 3 (solid) | 5 (strong) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conciseness | Rambles, over 2 minutes | Mostly 60–120 seconds | Tight 60–90 second answers |
| Structure | No clear point | Answer is there but buried | Leads with the answer, then one example |
| Relevance | Off-topic | Mostly on-topic | Clearly ties to the role |
| Delivery | Monotone or reading a script | Clear but stiff | Natural, steady pace, confident |
| Salary & logistics | Avoids or gives a low number | Gives a range | Gives a researched range confidently |

## Grading

| Criteria | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Completed all three screens (two in class, one on your own) | 40 |
| Observed and scored classmates' screens | 20 |
| Reflection covers all 5 questions | 30 |
| Re-run shows a concrete improvement | 10 |
