## Why you should know this

Your resume is read twice: first by software, then (if it gets through) by a person skimming for a few seconds. Both now involve AI. At the same time, many listings you'll find aren't real jobs at all — and some are scams. Today you'll tighten your resume for both readers and build a target list of real roles worth your time.

## Learning Objectives

1. Explain how ATS software and AI ranking tools process resumes, and what that means for formatting and wording

1. Compare AI resume feedback with human feedback and decide what to act on
1. Identify entry-level role types beyond software engineer and spot ghost postings and job scams

## Overview: How Resumes Get Read (20 min)

- **ATS** (applicant tracking system — software like Greenhouse, Lever, or Workday that stores applications): recruiters search and filter by keywords, titles, and locations. Some ATS tools now use AI to summarize or rank resumes against the job description.
- **What that means for you:**
  - Simple, single-column layout; standard headings; PDF that copies cleanly to plain text
  - Honest overlap with the 3–4 core requirements in the posting, in the posting's words
  - Don't keyword-stuff — a human reads it next
- **The human skim (about 6 seconds):** name and title → most recent role or project → skills → does anything stand out? A live project link helps a lot.
- **AI-sounding resumes are now a red flag.** Recruiters see hundreds of near-identical AI-written resumes. Plain verbs and real numbers beat "spearheaded impactful cross-functional initiatives."

Show two example bullets and rewrite them together:

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| Leveraged cutting-edge technologies to spearhead a robust full-stack solution | Built a meal-planning app with React and Postgres used by 25 classmates; deployed on Render |
| Passionate team player who drove impactful results | Fixed 12 bugs in a team project in 2 weeks and wrote tests that caught 3 regressions before demo day |

## Activity: Resume Hot Seat (25 min)

One at a time (about 8 minutes each), project a student's resume:

1. **6-second test:** everyone looks for 6 seconds, then says what they remember.
1. **Bullet rewrite:** the group picks the weakest bullet and rewrites it together live.
1. **Plain text test:** copy the PDF into a text editor. Does it come through in order?

## Activity: AI Critique vs. Human Critique (20 min)

1. Each student pastes their resume and a real job posting into an AI tool:

   ```text
   Here's a job posting and my resume. List the three most important
   requirements in the posting. For each one, tell me whether my resume shows
   clear evidence of it. Then point out any phrasing that sounds generic or
   AI-written. Don't rewrite my resume.
   ```

1. Compare the AI's feedback with the hot seat feedback. Where did they agree? What did the AI miss? What did it get wrong?
1. Decide on three changes you'll make. **You** write the edits.

**Discussion:** When is AI resume help useful, and when does it make your resume worse?

## Overview: Roles Beyond SWE, Ghost Postings & Scams (25 min)

**Roles to consider** (define each as you go):

- Frontend, backend, or full-stack developer
- **QA** (quality assurance) and **SDET** (software development engineer in test)
- Solutions engineer, support engineer, technical account manager
- **FDE** (forward-deployed engineer)
- Developer advocate / developer relations
- AI implementation, automation, or "AI engineer" roles at non-tech companies
- Contract and contract-to-hire roles (we'll cover how to evaluate these in Session 13)

**Spotting ghost postings** (listings for jobs that aren't actively being filled):

- Posted for 60+ days, or reposted repeatedly
- No named team, manager, or specific project
- Company has had recent layoffs or a hiring freeze
- Can't find the role on the company's own careers page
- **Best check:** ask someone who works there

**Spotting job scams** (often AI-powered now):

- Interviews only by text chat, or an offer without a real interview
- Requests for money, bank details, or your Social Security number before a signed offer
- Being asked to download and run unknown code as a "take-home" — it may be malware
- Email domains that don't match the company, or recruiters with brand-new profiles
- "Too good to be true" pay for little experience

## Activity: Build and Verify Your Target Company List (30 min)

1. Expand your target list to **15 companies**, sorted into tiers (dream / realistic / safe), with at least 3 startups or small companies.
1. For each company, find one real, current posting (or note "no current posting — networking target").
1. Run the ghost-posting checks on at least 5 of them.
1. Note any ACS alumni or contacts at each company — the instructor will help identify warm connections.

**AI tip:** ask an AI tool to suggest companies similar to ones you like ("companies like Notion with under 500 employees that hire junior developers"). Verify every suggestion — AI tools often name companies that are out of date, have shrunk, or don't exist.
