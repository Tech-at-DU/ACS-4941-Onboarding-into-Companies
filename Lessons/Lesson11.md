## Why you should know this

Today brings together several things interviewers evaluate in final rounds: explaining a design, handling tough follow-up questions, and communicating like someone who'll work well on a remote or hybrid team. Then you'll switch sides. Sitting on a simulated hiring committee — the group that reviews a candidate's whole packet and decides — shows you exactly which signals matter and which don't.

## Learning Objectives

1. Present a system design and defend its tradeoffs under questioning
1. Handle harder behavioral follow-up questions without losing structure
1. Communicate effectively in remote and asynchronous settings
1. Answer questions about how you use AI with specificity and judgment
1. Evaluate candidate packets the way a hiring committee does, and apply those lessons to your own

## Standup (10 min)

What I did · What I'll do · Where I'm stuck. How did the pipeline go over the break? Outreach and informational interview logs due today.

## System Design Walkthroughs (60 min)

Each student presents their [System Design Exercise](../Assignments/System_Design.md) (20 minutes each):

- **13 minutes:** present requirements, data model, API, diagram, and tradeoffs
- **7 minutes:** questions from the instructor and classmates — "What breaks first?" "Why not X instead?" and for Option C, "How would you know the answers are right?"

Classmates write down one question each before the presentation ends. Being in the audience is practice too — asking good questions is part of being a good engineer.

## Overview: Harder Behavioral Questions & Remote Communication (20 min)

**Handling follow-ups.** Interviewers dig into stories to check they're real and see your judgment:

- "What would you do differently?"
- "What did your teammate think about that?"
- "What if that hadn't worked?"
- "How did you know it was successful?"

The best defense is a true story you've reflected on. Prepare one "what I'd do differently" for each story in your bank.

**Remote and async communication** (asynchronous = not in real time: messages, docs, recorded updates):

- Write messages that can be understood without a follow-up: context, question, what you've tried, what you need
- Over-communicate progress and blockers — on remote teams, silence looks like inactivity
- Know when to switch from chat to a quick call
- Camera, audio, and lighting matter in video interviews and meetings

**Talking about AI in behavioral interviews.** Almost every company now asks some version of "How do you use AI in your work?" Strong answers are specific: which tool, what for, how you verify, and when you *don't* use it. See the [AI question bank](../Resources/Behavioral_Interview_Guide.md#ai-and-how-you-work).

## Rapid-Fire Drills (15 min)

Each student answers 3 questions in a row, 60 seconds each, with one follow-up from the instructor. Mix from:

- How do you use AI tools in your development work?
- Tell me about a time an AI tool gave you a wrong answer.
- When do you choose not to use AI?
- How do you stay visible and productive when working remotely?
- Tell me about a misunderstanding that happened over chat. How did you fix it?
- How do you decide when to ask for help?

Classmates give a thumbs up/sideways/down on each answer and one word of feedback.

## Hiring Committee Simulation (45 min)

**Setup (instructor prep):** the instructor prepares 5 candidate packets for one junior developer role with 2 openings: the 3 students' packets plus 2 fictional packets created by the instructor (one strong on paper but with an AI-generated project the candidate couldn't explain in their interview notes; one with a modest resume but a strong deployed project and thoughtful `DECISIONS.md`). Each packet contains: resume, live project URL, `DECISIONS.md`, and interview notes or one recorded behavioral answer. Students review all packets including their own.

1. **Individual review (15 min):** score each packet 1–4 on: evidence of skill, communication, judgment, and fit for the role. Write one sentence per packet: "Hire because…" or "No hire because…"
1. **Committee discussion (20 min):** discuss each packet, starting with the fictional ones. Agree on the 2 hires. The instructor plays the hiring manager and pushes back: "What evidence do we actually have that this person can do the job?"
1. **Reflection (10 min):** What signals mattered most? What did you skip over? What surprised you? What will you change in your own packet tonight?
