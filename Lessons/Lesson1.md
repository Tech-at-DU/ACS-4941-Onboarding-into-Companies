## Why you should know this

The job market you're entering is not the one most online advice describes. Entry-level roles are fewer and more competitive, AI has changed how resumes are screened and how interviews are run, and cold applications rarely work. Students who understand the current landscape and treat the search like a structured project get hired faster. This course is a 7-week sprint to build that system together.

## Learning Objectives

1. Describe how hiring has changed in 2026/2027, including the role of AI on both the employer and candidate sides
1. Identify a range of entry-level role types to target, beyond "Software Engineer"
1. Draft a personal job search plan with weekly, measurable targets
1. Set up a job search tracker and use it to report progress at standup
1. Establish a baseline on a timed algorithm problem

## Overview: The 2026/2027 Job Search (25 min)

Key points:

- **Fewer junior openings, more applicants per opening.** AI tools make it easy for anyone to apply to hundreds of jobs, so each posting gets flooded.
- **Ghost postings are common** — listings for jobs that aren't really being filled. We'll learn to spot them in Session 3.
- **Referrals and relationships beat volume.** A referred candidate is many times more likely to get an interview than a cold applicant.
- **Interview formats have multiplied:** algorithm problems, take-homes, live debugging, code review, AI-assisted coding rounds, and AI-run screening interviews.
- **Proof of work matters more than ever.** Anyone can generate a project; fewer people can show a deployed one and explain every decision.
- **Look beyond the "Software Engineer" (SWE) title.** Other good entry points include:
  - **QA** (quality assurance — testing software to find bugs) and **SDET** (software development engineer in test — writing code that tests software automatically)
  - **Solutions or support engineer** — technical problem-solving with customers
  - **FDE** (forward-deployed engineer) — building custom solutions with customers, common at AI companies
  - **Developer advocate**, **AI implementation** roles, and **contract or contract-to-hire** positions

**Discussion:** What have you already noticed in your own search? What's working, what isn't?

## Overview & Discussion: AI on Both Sides of Hiring (20 min)

Read together: [AI in the Job Search, Sections 1–3](../Resources/AI_In_The_Job_Search.md).

- **How employers use AI:** resume screening, AI-run first-round interviews, cheating detection, identity checks.
- **How candidates use AI well:** practice interviews, resume critique, research, explaining code.
- **How candidates misuse AI:** AI-sounding resumes and outreach, mass auto-apply tools, projects they can't explain, and hidden interview "assistant" tools that get people blacklisted.

**Think-pair-share:** Each person writes down one way they've used AI in their job search so far. Share it, and as a group decide: helpful, risky, or crossing the line?

## Challenge 1: Diagnostic Algorithm Problem (40 min)

A low-stakes baseline — this is about finding where you're starting, not about passing.

- Choose one problem from the **Warm-up** section of the [Problem Bank](../Resources/Problem_Bank.md).
- 35 minutes, no AI, talk through your approach in writing as comments before you code.
- 5 minutes: write down the time and space complexity and what you'd do differently.
- Submit your solution link in your tracker. We'll debrief on Wednesday.
