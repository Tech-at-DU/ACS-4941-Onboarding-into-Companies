## Why you should know this

Your first salary is the base every future raise and offer builds on. Most employers expect a polite negotiation, and a respectful ask almost never costs you an offer. Just as important: in this market you may see startup equity, contract roles, return-to-office requirements, and visa questions. Knowing how to evaluate those protects you from a job that looks good on paper but isn't.

## Learning Objectives

1. Research a realistic salary range and set walk-away, target, and anchor numbers
1. Handle the salary question in early screens, including AI-run screens
1. Evaluate an offer's full package: salary, equity, benefits, contract terms, RTO policy, and company stability
1. Explain key work authorization options (OPT, STEM OPT, H-1B) and what to ask employers
1. Negotiate an offer respectfully and confidently

## Overview: Knowing Your Numbers & the Early Salary Question (25 min)

Walk through [Sections 1–2 of the Salary Negotiation Guide](../Resources/Salary_Negotiation_Guide.md):

- **Three numbers:** walk-away, target, anchor — each backed by real sources.
- **Sources:** Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, salary ranges in job postings (required by law in California and several other states), and your network.
- **AI and salary research:** AI tools are useful for organizing what you've found, but often give outdated or invented salary figures. Always check a real source.
- **The early salary question:** recruiters and AI screens often ask in the first call. Practice the scripts out loud, one each, right now.

## Overview: Evaluating the Full Offer (20 min)

Walk through [Sections 3–4 of the guide](../Resources/Salary_Negotiation_Guide.md#3-evaluating-an-offer-the-full-package):

- **Equity:** stock options vs. RSUs (restricted stock units), vesting schedules, the 1-year cliff. Treat startup options like a lottery ticket, not salary.
- **Contract and contract-to-hire:** W-2 vs. 1099, converting an hourly rate to an annual figure, and asking hard questions about conversion.
- **RTO** (return to office): how many days on-site, and is it likely to change?
- **Stability:** recent layoffs, funding runway (how many months of money a startup has left).
- **Learning budget and AI tools:** will they pay for courses, conferences, and the AI tools you'll use?

**Quick exercise:** compare two sample offers on the board — a $105k salaried role vs. a $55/hour 1099 contract-to-hire. Which is worth more, and what questions would you ask about each?

## Overview: Work Authorization for International Students (20 min)

Walk through [Section 6 of the guide](../Resources/Salary_Negotiation_Guide.md#6-work-authorization-for-international-students). Define each term:

- **F-1:** the U.S. student visa
- **OPT** (Optional Practical Training): 12 months of work authorization after graduation
- **STEM OPT:** a 24-month extension for STEM degrees — the employer must use E-Verify and sign a training plan
- **H-1B:** the main long-term work visa, issued through an annual lottery; rules and fees changed significantly in 2025
- **Cap-gap:** may extend work authorization between OPT ending and H-1B starting

**What to ask employers:** "Does the company sponsor visas for this role?" Ask early, ideally in the first screen.

**Instructor note:** invite someone from the international student office to join for this segment if possible, and confirm current rules before class. This segment is useful for every student — even those who don't need sponsorship will work alongside colleagues who do.

## Live Negotiation Role-Plays (45 min)

Each student (15 minutes each) receives a random scenario from the [Negotiation assignment](../Assignments/Negotiation.md#part-2-live-negotiation-role-play-in-class). The instructor plays the recruiter:

1. Recruiter calls with the offer (5 min). Student thanks them, asks questions, asks for time.
1. Student calls back with their ask (5 min).
1. Feedback from classmates and instructor (5 min): Did they thank, ask for time, make a reasoned ask, and avoid accepting on the spot?

## Peer Role-Plays With AI-Generated Scenarios (15 min)

In pairs (the instructor joins as the third), ask an AI tool to generate a tricky scenario for your partner:

```text
Create a realistic job offer scenario for a junior developer to practice
negotiating. Include the company type, base salary, one unusual element
(like equity, contract terms, or an RTO requirement), and one surprise the
recruiter will reveal partway through. Don't include advice.
```

Play the recruiter for each other. Practice using the scripts from [Section 5 of the guide](../Resources/Salary_Negotiation_Guide.md#5-how-to-negotiate).
